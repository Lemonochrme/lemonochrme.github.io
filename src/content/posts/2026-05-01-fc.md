# Designing a STM32F405 Drone Flight Controller

This post documents the design choices behind a custom flight controller for a cinelifter drone intended to carry a Sony A7S III. Everything is built from scratch: frame, ESCs with custom FreeRTOS firmware, and the FC described here.


## MCU: STM32F405

**Why STM32?** It's pretty simple we're based in France and ST Microelectronics is a French-Italian company. Could we have used an AT32 clone? Sure. Did we? No.

**Why the F405 specifically?** Enough compute headroom for sensor fusion, OSD, USB, and 4+ UARTs, without jumping to an H7 and all the complexity that comes with it. 168 MHz Cortex-M4 with FPU is more than sufficient.

## IMU Stack: Dual Sensor

We went with two IMUs:

**ICM-42688-P (U2)** Primary IMU, connected on SPI1 with its own dedicated chip select. This is the workhorse: low noise, high ODR, the standard choice for performance flight controllers. It handles all active stabilization.

**BNO085 (U1)** Secondary IMU, on SPI3. The BNO085 has an integrated sensor fusion processor (rotation vectors, game RV, ARVR stabilized outputs). We wired it up fully -- INT, RST, PS0/WAKE, CS -- but are not using its fusion output yet. The ICM runs everything for now; the BNO085 is there for future use.

**Then geopolitics intervened.** The war in Ukraine disrupted supply chains badly enough that ICM-42688-P stock dried up. We sourced what we thought were equivalent Chinese clones. They were not pin-compatible. Lesson learned: always validate a clone against the datasheet before committing to a BOM. Next revision, we're qualifying a proper alternative before tapeout.


## Barometer: LPS22HH

U3 is an ST LPS22HH barometric pressure sensor, also on SPI2. Standard choice for altitude hold. Connected with its own CS and INT line to the MCU.


## OSD: AT7456E

U4 is the AT7456ELAH analog OSD chip, a classic for FPV. It sits on SPI2 (shared with the baro and flash) and has three independent power domains:

- DVDD: 3.3V (filtered via L4)
- AVDD: 5V (filtered via L1)
- PVDD: 5V (filtered via L2)

Video in and out are AC-coupled through resistor networks (R15/R16 on the input side, R10 on output). The MCU drives reset directly.


## Flash: SPI NOR for Blackbox

U5 is a SOIC-8 SPI NOR flash for blackbox logging, also on SPI2. Standard. Nothing to add.


## Power Architecture

**Battery input** feeds directly to a voltage divider (R24/R26) into an ADC pin on the MCU for battery voltage sensing.

**5V Buck (U6)** -- A switching regulator (10uH inductor L3, 22uF output caps C19/C20) generates 5V for the OSD power domains and other 5V consumers. Feedback network uses R18/R19/R20/R21.

**5V from USB (D1)** -- A diode ORs USB 5V with the buck output so the board can be powered over USB without a battery.

**3.3V LDO (U7)** -- Generates 3.3V for the MCU, IMUs, flash, baro, and logic. Exposed as a castellated pad for external use.

**And then there was the VDDA incident.** One VDDA pin on the STM32 was left unconnected to 3V3 in the layout. The MCU simply refused to enumerate on STM32CubeIDE. After an embarrassing amount of debugging time, the fix was a bodge wire bridging two adjacent decoupling caps. Lesson: double-check every power pin in the MCU datasheet, including the analog supply. It is now on the v2 checklist.


## Connectivity

**UARTs (5x):**
- UART2: RC receiver (RX/TX exposed as castellated pads RRX/RTX)
- UART3: General peripheral (RX3/TX3)
- UART4: General peripheral (RX4/TX4)
- UART5: Debug TX (TX5)
- USART1: (OSC-derived clock path via X2 external oscillator)

**USB:** Full-speed USB via USB-C connector (6-pin, no CC resistors for power role, just data). 22-ohm series resistors on D+/D- (R4/R7), pull-up on VBUS detect (R3/R6).

**Motor outputs:** S1-S4 signals (castellated pads) drive ESCs.

**SWD:** SWCLK and SWDIO exposed for programming and debug.

**I2C:** SCL/SDA exposed as castellated pads for external peripherals (GPS with compass, etc.).

**RSSI:** Dedicated ADC input with RC filter (R25, C32).

**Buzzer:** Direct GPIO drive.

## Indicators and Misc

Two RGB-capable status LEDs (STATE1 green, STATE2 blue) driven via GPIO through current-limiting resistors. Two power indicator LEDs (3.3V and 5V rails).

Seven test points (TP1-TP7) on key MCU signals for bring-up probing.

BOOT0 button (a 4-pad tactile switch) pulls BOOT0 high for DFU mode. In v1, the footprint had a connectivity issue and the button did not work. A solder bridge fixed it in the field. Fixed in v2.

## Bring-Up Results

The boards were assembled and powered up. Summary:

- USB enumeration: working (after VDDA bodge)
- SWD/STM32CubeIDE: working (after VDDA bodge)
- OSD: working
- Flash: working
- BNO085: working
- ICM-42688-P: failed (clone parts, non-conformant -- see IMU section above)
- Boot button: non-functional (fixed with solder bridge)
- UART, GPIO, PWM outputs: validated

Overall the board works. The two hardware bugs were minor and both were recoverable with bodge wires. The IMU situation is a supply chain problem, not a design problem.

## Next Version: Integrated AIO

V2 will put everything on a single PCB: 4 ESCs + FC, targeting a 380mm diagonal frame. Goals are weight reduction, cleaner signal routing between FC and ESCs, and elimination of all inter-board connectors. The IMU will be re-sourced from a qualified supplier. The VDDA and BOOT0 bugs will obviously not make it into v2.

More on that when the layout is done.
