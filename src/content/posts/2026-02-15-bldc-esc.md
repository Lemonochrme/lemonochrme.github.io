# Designing a Sensorless BLDC ESC

LithDrive is a sensorless three-phase BLDC ESC I designed from scratch. I handled the schematic, PCB layout, assembly and bring-up.

<img width="3000" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/35e4f3f4-35f3-410b-a81c-1b1e0998ca17" />

## Context

It started as the motor driver for **Nanolith**, an open-source 3-inch drone. The idea: merge frame, flight controller and ESCs into a single PCB. **LithCore** handles flight control, **LithDrive** handles motor power. Each subsystem gets validated on its own before integration.

Midway through, a friend joined the project and we changed the scope to a **6S cinelifter**. The board below is the 2S validation prototype.

## Architecture

- **MCU:** STM32F051C8T6, AM32-compatible firmware
- **Gate driver:** DRV8300
- **Power stage:** 3 half-bridges, 6 × PSMN1R4-40YLDX-VB N-MOSFETs (oversized on purpose for bring-up margin), 20 Ω gate resistors
- **High-side supply:** bootstrap diode + capacitor per phase
- **Regulation:** VBAT → 5 V → 3.3 V
- **Current sensing:** 1 mΩ low-side shunt + INA180
- **Voltage sensing:** resistor divider
- **Commutation:** back-EMF zero-crossing on BEMFA/B/C against a BEMF_COMMON reference

## Layout

The board mixes high-current switching and small-signal analog sensing. My priorities:

- Short high-current loops
- Phase nodes away from analog inputs
- Tight gate traces
- BEMF lines protected from switching noise

## Bring-up

I assembled in stages: MCU, decoupling and regulators first. Once SWD and flashing worked, I added the power stage. That made faults much easier to isolate.

- SWD connected, bootloader and firmware flashed, config tool connected
- Motor spun in open loop on first try
- Closed loop was bad: heavy cogging, unstable startup

The command signal was also suspect. I burned my servo tester, then replaced it with an ESP32-C3 generating 50 Hz PWM. That signal was unreliable at first, so I fixed it before debugging the ESC.

## Bugs found

1. **Duplicated net label.** I copied BEMFB and never renamed it to BEMFC. Two dividers shared one net, and the MCU's BEMFC input was left unconnected. I patched it with magnet wire under the microscope.
2. **Missing junction.** Only one of the three BEMF_COMMON resistors was actually tied to the node. The junction dot was missing in the schematic. A small wire fixed it.
3. **Bad solder joint.** PB4 looked dead and blocked flashing. The PCB trace was fine; the MCU lead wasn't soldered. A reflow fixed it.

After fix #2, the motor ran smoothly with no cogging.
